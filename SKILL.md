# Medical PBL slides

Produce the finished editable PPTX, not merely a research brief. Input is normally a topic/question plus a teaching-unit PDF. Communicate in Traditional Chinese; slides are English; notes use Traditional Chinese with English medical terms. The audience is preclinical medical students with no clinical-rotation experience. Teach only the prerequisites and causal links needed to answer the assigned question. Presenter notes are minimal plain-text cues for quick scanning while speaking.

Use this user's latest explicit instructions over these defaults. Do not invent case facts or hard-code the example patient's disease into a new task. Do not impose 10 questions, 28 slides, a mandatory cover, or an approval/upload checkpoint. A connected university VPN is a possible access route, not proof of full-text access.

## Route by task

- **New topic + unit PDF:** read [reasoning.md](references/reasoning.md), then [literature-access.md](references/literature-access.md). Build the evidence/slide plan before authoring. Read [presentation.md](references/presentation.md) at production time.
- **Content/notes revision:** inspect the latest actual deck and its notes, then read the relevant sections of reasoning and presentation. Search only for newly needed medical evidence.
- **Style/citation-only revision:** inspect the user's edited reference slide and latest files; read presentation only. Do not restart literature research or rewrite clinical content.
- **Implementation and recovery:** [operations.md](references/operations.md) contains tested package-editing rules, validation, scripts and bounded access retries.

Use the available presentation/PDF capability for actual rendering and export. Resolve its installed paths and runtime dynamically; do not assume a cached version number. No skill-specific external connector is required. Do not load unrelated PBL pipelines with conflicting styles merely because their names match.

## Essential workflow

1. Inspect available files and the provided PDF. Establish the question's scope and **time point**. Separate known, absent, unreported, inferred and later-revealed findings, with PDF page pointers. Do not use the unit's eventual answer as evidence available at initial presentation.
2. Build a host model and a sequence of small clinical questions. For each proposed test record: why this host needs it, expected finding, what a positive means, what a negative means, limitations, and next decision. Distinguish compatibility from confirmation and coexistence from mutually exclusive diagnoses.
3. Actively search PubMed and the wider web by these questions. Broaden the literature search across the relevant mechanisms, diagnostic distinctions and clinical decisions; compare multiple complementary papers rather than relying on one or two general reviews. Seek relevant reviews for the clinical framework and mechanisms, plus guidelines and primary studies for specific claims. Expand the selected paper collection across these complementary roles, including relevant supplementary reading, and inspect full texts for original mechanism diagrams, pathways, flowcharts and diagnostic/treatment algorithms. Do not stop once the minimum sources for text claims have been found; continue targeted searches for useful visual explanations and unresolved questions, then stop when those needs are covered; do not impose a paper or image quota or add irrelevant material to increase counts. Also consider trustworthy web articles and educational resources for clearer visual explanations; compare evidence quality, explanatory logic and relevance rather than selecting by prestige, recency or attractive artwork alone. Download every selected paper whose PDF is accessible into the task workspace’s `papers/` folder, including supplementary reading; do not stop at links, abstracts or a request for uploads when retrieval is possible. Follow [literature-access.md](references/literature-access.md) for retrieval, failed-access reporting and source records. Read the full texts and captions used in the deck. Never claim a formal systematic review without its methodology.
   Also search Taiwan physician licensing past exams for questions testing the same concept or reasoning just taught. Include only high-overlap questions, immediately after the corresponding concept; omit them when no good match is found. Verify the original question and official answer, and follow the exam-slide format and selection rules in [presentation.md](references/presentation.md).
4. Save a compact evidence/slide plan using [plan.schema.json](assets/plan.schema.json). Track decisions once rather than repeatedly rediscovering them. Use a source ID across the plan, papers, figures and citations.
5. Draft one reasoning move per slide, planning the visual before the prose. Carry a clear thread from the case question through relevant mechanisms and evidence to the answer: each slide resolves a question or creates the need for the next. Remove or merge material that does not advance this thread; do not add slides merely to display collected sources. Actively seek more useful images across the expanded paper collection and trustworthy educational resources. Use original mechanism/flow figures, imaging, meaningful crops, plots and decision diagrams to explain causal links, findings and decisions. Also use actual medical equipment, drug/product photos and anatomical or molecular structures to make the items being discussed concrete. A relevant contextual image may accompany a slide even when it is not the main teaching point or a paper figure—for example, show an oxygen device while introducing that device. Distinguish contextual illustration from clinical evidence, verify the depicted item, and cite its actual source. Make the deck substantially visual rather than relying on pure-text slides; avoid unrelated or repeated filler. Add a modest amount of explanatory text so the audience can see the premise, causal link and implication on the slide itself, rather than isolated keywords; keep concise speaking cues in notes. Use a cited editable explanatory diagram when the literature has no suitable original figure; never invent clinical data. Avoid a deck dominated by tables, repeated text columns or bullet summaries. Teach necessary prerequisites before algorithms and put key interpretation cues in notes. Follow the visual selection and crop checks in [presentation.md](references/presentation.md).
6. Author in the style below. Write minimal plain-text notes that let the speaker scan all key points at a glance during the presentation, following reasoning.md. Keep full bibliographic entries visible in one continuous final References list, without topic/source categories. Use one body text box per reference page; fill the first page at a legible size before continuing overflow entries onto the next. Follow the pagination rules in [presentation.md](references/presentation.md).
7. Render, inspect, repair, verify note-to-slide correspondence and source mapping, then deliver a versioned PPTX. Preserve the user's edited source. Deliver links to both the versioned PPTX and `papers/`, with a brief, accurate count and any material access gaps; do not claim every sought paper was downloaded if some failed.

## Non-negotiable defaults from the approved final deck

- White background. Burgundy title `#800020`; body `#202124`; **selected inline keywords** `#5A8298`; citations `#5F6368`.
- No subtitle/step kicker, no `Next:` subquestion, no page numbers, **no bottom green takeaway bar**. Do not resurrect earlier orange/sage designs. Original paper artwork keeps its own colors.
- Teaching slides use one main title that names the slide’s specific mechanism, finding, test or clinical decision; use a question only when it expresses the concrete question answered by this slide. Follow the title guidance in [presentation.md](references/presentation.md). Pair it with a sourced explanatory visual or a relevant equipment/drug/structure image and concise explanatory statements, or interpreted text/table. Functional body labels may reveal the reasoning structure; they are not decorative subtitles. Relevant real medical object photos and sourced anatomical/molecular illustrations are welcome even when contextual. Avoid unrelated stock pictures, fabricated AI medical images and filler icons.
- Figure citations: `Li et al. (2025), Figure 4E`. A crop is still cited by the original figure/panel; do not automatically prepend “Modified from” or “Reproduced from”. Use an adaptation disclosure only for substantive redrawing/recombination/changed scientific content. Teaching handout: author/year, handout name, page.
- Web visuals cite the specific article/resource with an author or organisation, date when available, and a separate clickable `Link` anchor or URL; do not cite an image-search results page. Short citations fit on **one visible footer line**. Complete APA entries belong in the continuous final References list, with one body text box per page and overflow only when the preceding page is full at a legible size, not only in notes. Text synthesis may say “Synthesized from” when useful; it is not a claim of generated artwork. In both footer citations and final References, only the visible URL/DOI or a dedicated `Link` anchor has an underlined hyperlink. Author/year, title, figure label and all other citation text stay plain and unlinked. See presentation.md for run-level checks.
- Notes are minimal plain-text presentation cues in Traditional Chinese with English medical terms. Use short lines in speaking order so the presenter can scan all key points at a glance: the main point, essential causal link or figure/test interpretation, case implication, necessary caution and a brief transition when useful. No long prose, full script, Markdown formatting, or appended reading article. Preserve the reasoning and uncertainty in concise language; do not compress them into disconnected keywords. This replaces the former two-part cheat-sheet-plus-article requirement.

## Helpers

Run scripts with the resolved Python environment; each has `--help`.
- `scripts/fetch_pdf.py`: bounded download to a new local file, PDF signature/parser validation and provenance JSON. For public or institution-IP-authorized URLs; browser-authenticated downloads use the browser instead.
- `scripts/crop_figure.py`: render/crop a PDF region at adequate resolution and record exact source/page/bounds. No invented or AI-redrawn medical images.
- `scripts/deck_notes.py`: extract or replace notes using actual slide relationships; preserves slide artwork. Replacement requires a complete, matching slide list.
- `scripts/audit_deck.py`: detect missing notes, old template artifacts and citation formatting. A structural check, not medical or visual QA.

These tools reduce repetitive work. They do not replace source reading, clinical judgment, checking captions, or inspecting the rendered deck.
